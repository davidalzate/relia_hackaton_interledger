  **Hard violations of documented standards**

  2. **Cursor format.** In `SimulatedInbox.cs:58-70` the cursor is the raw
  message id (`page[^1].Id.ToString(...)`). The existing paginated service,
  `AgentService.cs:80` `HistoryCursor`, uses an opaque base64url cursor. Two

  3. **Where mappings live.** `Services/Mapping.cs` says it holds entity-to-DTO
  mappings "compartido por los servicios". `SimulatedInbox.ToDto` and its

  **Standards review (backend, uncommitted against 3cf3524)**

  **Hard violations (documented standards)**
  - `API/Errors/ApiErrors.cs` `AmountTooLow()`: the user-facing message "El
  monto no alcanza a cubrir la **tarifa**" uses a word GLOSSARY.md bans. Under
  **Comisión Relia**, "tarifa" is listed as `_Avoid_`. It should say "comisión".

  That is the only hard breach. "Billetera" is used correctly in the deposit
  notice text and in the descriptions. The comments say "proxy" (glossary entry
  **Adaptador de riel** lists `_Avoid_: proxy`), but ADR 0001 and the existing
  RailClient also say proxy, so this is a judgement call.

  **Judgement calls (baseline smells)**
  - *Duplicated Code*: `OperationService.CreateDepositAsync` repeats `requested
  <= Math.Max(fee.Total, OperationRules.MinimumRequestedAmount)` and the
  quota-available calculation from `OperationRules.Preview`. Extract one shared
  check in OperationRules that both use.
  - *Primitive Obsession / Shotgun Surgery*: the failure codes
  `"customer_rejected"`… are string literals in `RailStatusTranslation`, in the
  keys of `Mapping.FailureCodes` and in `ck_op_failure_code`. Rail statuses
  (`"pending_approval"`…) and the `action` values `"create"`/`"refresh"` are
  bare strings too, even though `RailDirection` got constants in this same diff.
  - *Repeated Switches*: there are now three switches on `OperationKind`:
  `Mapping.ToDto`, `OperationSync.Posting` and `AgentService`'s labels. Each
  falls back to a silent default (`_ => QuotaTopUp`, `_ => Cancelled`).
  - *Mysterious Name*: in `Mapping.ToDto` there is a bare positional `null`
  between `ApprovalUrl` and `CashHandedOverAt`. Use a named argument. In
  `OperationService`, the parameters `ledgerEntries` and `ledger` are easy to
  confuse.
  - *Inconsistency*: `RefreshAsync` says the rail call stays outside the
  transaction so no rows are locked. `CreateDepositAsync`, however, holds
  `agent_balances FOR UPDATE` across the HTTP POST. That may be needed for the
  reservation, but it is not documented.
  - `RailRefreshThrottle` never evicts entries, so its dictionary grows without
  bound.
  - In `CopyRailData`, `ErrorCode`/`ErrorMessage` are overwritten
  unconditionally, while the other fields keep their old value when the rail
  sends null (`?? payment.X`).

  **Bugs / risks**
  1. **Possible double payout (high).** `payment.Id = Guid.NewGuid()` is used as
  the rail Idempotency-Key. Suppose the POST times out (RailUnavailable → 503)
  after the adapter has already created the payment. The filter rolls back and
  the client retries with the same HTTP key. The retry gets a new payment.Id, so
  the rail creates a second outgoing payment. The key should be derived from
  the operation, or from the agent plus the idempotency key.

  already ended another way (e.g. expired by the backend) by recording the event

  `UpdatedAt` is generated by the database and read back, so `ToDtoAsync` is
  fine.

⏺ Ticket 09: Retiro, cancelación y entrega de efectivo is implemented and
  committed to dotnet-jeff as 67d5997. It's tested against a fake proxy and
  partly against the real one. A person still has to approve and reject
  withdrawals on the testnet, and the Postman collection hasn't been run.

  What works:
  - Withdrawal: POST /agents/{id}/withdrawals returns 202 with approvalUrl and
    expiresAt.
    - It reserves the net amount from cash, or returns insufficient_cash with
      shortfall.
    - It asks the proxy to charge the customer's wallet the charged amount, with
      a readable description.
    - The customer gets an SMS with the approval link, and the request shows up
      in GET /demo/payment-requests ("Mi billetera").
  - Outcomes:
    - Approved → completed: quota goes up by the net amount, cash goes down by
      the net amount, the agent's commission is added and the receipt is frozen.
    - rejected gives customer_rejected, expired gives request_expired, cancelled
      gives request_cancelled and failed gives payment_failed; each frees the
      reservation.
  - Cancel: 200 before the customer approves, and it also cancels on the rail.
    After approval, or when the proxy says it can't cancel, it returns 409
    operation_not_cancellable.
  - Cash handover: only for completed withdrawals, otherwise 409
    operation_not_completed. Repeating it returns the same cashHandedOverAt and
    records one event.
  - Admin: gets 403 on create, cancel and handover.
  - Real proxy: a $10.000 withdrawal got a real approval link from
    auth.interledger-test.dev, and cancelling it before approval worked.
  - Balances: every balance mismatch in v_agent_balance_check stays at 0.

  Changes you might notice:
  - Approval window: expiresAt is rounded up to the next whole minute, so the
    window is 5–6 minutes instead of exactly 5. The proxy treats a different
    expiresAt as a different request. Rounding means a retry after a timeout in
    the same minute gets the same payment back (tested). A retry that crosses
    the minute gets 409 idempotency_conflict and nobody is charged twice.
  - Stale approval links: once the customer approves, the operation stops
    showing the approval link and it drops out of "Mi billetera". Requests past
    their deadline drop out too.
  - Expiry: making the backend itself expire a withdrawal at expiresAt is
    ticket 11. Until then a withdrawal only expires when the proxy reports it.

⏺ Ticket 11: Barrido de fondo y vencimiento de solicitudes is implemented,
  verified by hand and committed to dotnet-jeff as 1c1e823. Only the Postman run
  is left.

  What works:
  - Background sweep: every 15 s (Relia:SweepIntervalSeconds) it refreshes every
    pending operation with the same logic as reading one. One failure doesn't
    stop the others, and the service doesn't crash.
  - Extension point: ticket 13's settlements can plug into the same sweep.
  - Expiry, when reading or sweeping: past expiresAt, the backend cancels the
    payment on the rail and leaves the operation expired/request_expired,
    freeing the reservation. Bre-B top-ups expire locally.
    - If the payer approved just in time, the operation continues and completes
      normally.
    - If the proxy is down, the operation stays pending and is retried on the
      next cycle.
    - If the proxy no longer knows the payment, the backend expires it.

  Verified:
  - Fake proxy (sweep every 3 s):
    - a deposit completed with no GET /operations and showed in history;
    - a withdrawal and a Bre-B top-up past their deadline became expired;
    - one approved just before the deadline completed;
    - with the proxy frozen the operation stayed pending, then expired once the
      proxy came back;
    - every balance mismatch stayed at 0.
  - Real proxy: a real $10.000 withdrawal (OP-000064) left unapproved became
    expired at 04:41:03, three seconds after its deadline, with nobody reading
    it. The proxy's own timer reported it first and the sweep applied it.

  Fixed during testing and review:
  - Cancelled instead of expired: if the proxy stops responding, a cancel the
    backend sent while expiring can still go through after the timeout. The next
    read then saw cancelled and marked the operation cancelled instead of
    expired. A rail cancelled past the deadline that the agent didn't request
    now counts as an expiry.
    - Edge case: an agent who cancels at 4:59 and gets a 503 would see expired
      instead of cancelled.
  - Retries: expiry no longer retries its rail cancel on every read (one attempt
    per second per payment), and permanent proxy errors no longer retry
    forever.
  - Payment log: it now records the right state, and expiry checks it is acting
    on the payment it read.

  Things to know:
  - Log noise: every refresh of a pending operation adds a payment log entry,
    one every 15 s per operation plus one per second while the frontend polls
    it. That's harmless, but the log grows during long waits.
  - Ticket 14's "expire now" endpoint can reuse this. It will need to tell apart
    "not pending", "payer already approved" and "the rail closed it first",
    which currently return the same result.
